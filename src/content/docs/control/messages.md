---
title: Text messages
description: Talking to another position, working a frequency channel, and the announcements the client sends for you.
---

The envelope on the menu bar opens **MESSAGES**. It holds two kinds of conversation:
private ones with another controller, and **frequency channels**, which everyone tuned to
that frequency reads.

When a message arrives while the box is closed, the envelope **blinks blue** until you
open it.

## Your frequency channel

Your own frequency is always there, at the top of the list, named after the frequency
itself — `121.430`. You cannot close it: it is not a correspondent you chose, it follows
the position you are holding.

Anything you send on it is read by everyone on that frequency, pilots included. It is the
written form of what you would say on the radio.

## Other frequencies

The network carries every frequency's traffic, but the box only shows the ones you asked
for. **+** in the conversation list, then type a frequency — `133.100` — and that channel
opens. It behaves like your own, except that you can close it again.

Type a callsign instead and you get a private conversation with that position.

Without this, the box would fill with the chatter of every frequency in the country.

## Announcements the client sends for you

Two actions put a message on your frequency channel by themselves, so the flight and
anyone listening see the instruction in writing:

| Action | Message |
|---|---|
| **Transfer** a flight to the next position | `TAR681, Contact LFML_TWR on 133.100, good day.` |
| **Release** a flight | `TVP7604, Switch to UNICOM 122.800, good day.` |

The transfer announcement needs the next position's frequency, which comes from the
network's ATC list. If that position is not online, the transfer still happens and the
announcement is simply not sent — better silence than *Contact LFML_TWR on …*.
